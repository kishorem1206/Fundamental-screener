# Fundamental Screener — Cash Flow Analysis Engine
## Standalone Implementation Specification

> **Scope:** This document is ONLY for Cash Flow Statement analysis.
>
> It is designed as a standalone module for the Fundamental Screener and should integrate with the existing P&L and Balance Sheet data layers where inputs are required, without duplicating their analysis.

---

# 1. Objective

Build a **Cash Flow Intelligence Engine**, not merely a page showing CFO, CFI and CFF.

The engine should answer:

- Is reported operating profit converting into actual cash?
- How much cash is being generated by the core business?
- Where is operating cash getting trapped?
- How much cash is being reinvested?
- Is the company generating Free Cash Flow?
- Is the company funding growth internally or externally?
- Is debt being raised or repaid?
- How much cash is being returned to shareholders?
- Why did the cash balance increase or decrease?
- Are unusual cash-flow patterns visible?
- Which cash-flow metrics are available from the current data?
- Which metrics require additional sources?

Core principle from the supplied framework:

> "Profit is on paper, cash is in hand."

The source emphasizes Cash Flow as a key test of whether reported operating performance is supported by realized cash. 

---

# 2. Three Core Cash Flow Pillars

The engine must separate the statement into:

```text
CFO = Cash Flow from Operating Activities
CFI = Cash Flow from Investing Activities
CFF = Cash Flow from Financing Activities
```

Conceptually:

```text
                  CASH FLOW STATEMENT

                         │
          ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
          ↓              ↓              ↓
        CFO             CFI            CFF
     Operations       Investing      Financing
          │              │              │
          ↓              ↓              ↓
     Core business    Capex/assets    Debt/equity/
     cash generation  investments     dividends
```

The supplied guide defines:

### CFO
Cash generated directly from core business operations, incorporating operating profit, working-capital changes and direct taxes. fileciteturn5file0L20-L27

### CFI
Cash spent on or received from capital investments, including fixed assets, Capital WIP, interest received and group investments. fileciteturn5file0L29-L35

### CFF
Cash flows related to capital structure, including borrowings, debt repayments, interest, dividends and equity issuance. fileciteturn5file0L36-L42

# 3. Sign Convention

The data-normalization layer must enforce one consistent internal sign convention.

## Positive

Cash inflow / cash retained:

```text
Customer cash collections
Debt raised
Equity issued
Payables increase
Asset/investment sale
Interest received
```

## Negative

Cash outflow / cash tied up:

```text
Inventory purchase
Receivables increase
Capex
Debt repayment
Interest paid
Dividend paid
Investment purchase
```

The source explicitly distinguishes cash inflows from cash outflows and treats conversion of cash into non-cash assets such as receivables/inventory as a cash outflow/tie-up. fileciteturn5file0L10-L19

### Important implementation rule

The raw-source sign should be preserved separately:

```text
raw_value
normalized_value
raw_sign
normalization_rule
```

Never overwrite the original source value.

```text
        +

        -
```

# 8. CFO / Operating Profit Conversion

Mandatory metric from the supplied framework:

```text
CFO / Operating Profit Ratio
=
(CFO / Operating Profit) × 100
```

The source uses a case example:

```text
55 / 135 = 37%
```

and identifies approximately 100% as the target conversion level. fileciteturn5file0L95-L126

Classify:

```text
>100%
~100%
50–100%
<50%
```

But preserve the actual percentage in the output.

---

# 9. CFO Conversion Interpretation

## Around 100%

Indicates that operating profit is broadly backed by realized operating cash.

## Above 100%

May indicate:

```text
collection of prior-period receivables
advance customer payments
working-capital release
```

The source gives examples above 100% and attributes strong conversion to factors such as collection of prior-period receivables or advance customer payables. fileciteturn5file0L127-L130

## Below 50%

Flag:

```text
LOW_CFO_CONVERSION
```

The source treats conversion below 50% as an earnings-quality red flag. fileciteturn5file0L114-L126

Important:

A single low-conversion period should not automatically be treated as evidence of a persistent problem.

# 13. Receivables Cash Drag

When receivables increase:

```text
Receivables Change < 0 cash-flow impact
```

show:

```text
Cash tied up in receivables
```

Cross-check:

```text
Receivables Growth
vs
Revenue Growth
```

If:

```text
Receivables Growth > Revenue Growth
```

flag:

```text
RECEIVABLE_CASH_DRAG
```

If this persists across periods:

```text
PERSISTENT_RECEIVABLE_CASH_DRAG
```

The supplied guide explicitly instructs the engine to cross-check rising receivables against sales growth. fileciteturn5file0L139-L141

---

# 14. Inventory Cash Drag

When inventory increases:

```text
Inventory Change < 0 cash-flow impact
```

show:

```text
Cash tied up in inventory
```

Cross-check:

```text
Inventory Growth
vs
Revenue / Demand Growth
```

If inventory rises while demand/activity is not keeping pace:

```text
INVENTORY_CASH_DRAG
```

The source states that higher inventory can be appropriate when demand is surging but can indicate inventory-obsolescence risk if turnover slows. fileciteturn5file0L143-L144

---

# 15. Payables Cash Support

When payables increase:

```text
Payables Change > 0
```

show:

```text
Supplier financing / cash retention
```

Do not automatically classify this as positive.

The engine must distinguish:

```text
normal supplier financing
vs
potential payment-pressure signal
```

using available AP aging and other data from the Balance Sheet module.

```text

```

```text

```

```text
55 - 35 = +20 Cr
```

as its case example. fileciteturn5file0L104-L113

```text
FCF
FCF Growth
FCF Margin
FCF / CFO

```

# 20. FCF Quality


# 21. Capex vs CFO

Calculate:

```text
Capex / CFO
```

Interpret:

```text
<100%
```

means CFO covers capex.

```text
>100%
```

means current-period CFO does not fully cover capex.

Then identify funding source through CFF:

```text
Debt
Equity
Cash reserves
```

This should answer:

> How is expansion being funded?


# 23. Other Investing Items

Capture separately:

```text
Other Investing Items
```

Do not bury them inside a generic CFI number.

```text
Other Investing Items / Annual CFO
```

```text

```

```text
UNALLOCATED_CAPITAL_DRAG
```

Only when the required disclosure/context is genuinely unavailable.

# 29. Net Cash Flow

Mandatory:

```text

CFI       -68
CFF         0
           ----
```

```text
Cash declined because investing outflows exceeded
```

# 38. Forensic Cash Flow Patterns

The engine should detect combinations rather than relying only on single ratios.

## Pattern A — Profit Without Cash

```text
Operating Profit ↑
CFO ↓
CFO Conversion ↓
```

```text
PROFIT_CASH_DIVERGENCE
```

```text

+

+

```

```text
RECEIVABLE_CASH_DRAG
```

---

## Pattern C — Inventory-Driven Cash Weakness

```text
Inventory ↑
+
CFO ↓
+
Inventory turnover deteriorating
```

```text

```

---

## Pattern D — Supplier-Financed CFO

```text
Payables ↑ materially
+
CFO ↑
```

Flag:

```text
PAYABLE_SUPPORTED_CFO
```

```text

```

```text

```


## Pattern G — Asset-Liquidation Cash Flow

```text
CFO / Operating Profit < 50%
```

```text

```

---

## Red Flag 2 — Unallocated Capital Drag

```text
Other Investing Items
>

```

# 68. Most Important Engineering Principle

Do not build this as:

```text
CFO
CFI
CFF
FCF
```

Build it as:

```text
PROFIT
   ↓
OPERATING CASH
   ↓
WORKING CAPITAL DRIVERS
   ↓
REINVESTMENT
   ↓
FREE CASH FLOW
   ↓
FINANCING DECISIONS
   ↓
CHANGE IN CASH
```

The purpose is to explain **how accounting earnings became—or failed to become—cash, where that cash went, and how the company funded its capital requirements**.

The supplied Cash Flow guide specifically frames CFO as the foundational earnings-quality test, requires multi-period analysis, and calls for automated alerts around low cash conversion and unexplained investing outflows. fileciteturn5file0L47-L48 fileciteturn5file0L133-L151
